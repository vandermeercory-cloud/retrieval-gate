## 1. Pattern day trader (PDT) rule: status as of October 2026, cash accounts and good-faith violations

### Takeaway
The PDT designation and the $25,000 minimum are gone. The SEC approved FINRA's amendments to Rule 4210 in mid-April 2026, and they took effect on **June 4, 2026**. Firms that need system changes have until **October 20, 2027**. An "intraday margin" standard replaces PDT: maintenance margin now applies throughout the trading day in margin accounts, and the $2,000 minimum equity still applies to any leveraged trading. Cash accounts were never subject to PDT. They are still governed by settlement and Regulation T, so T+1 settlement, good-faith violations and free-riding still apply. Robinhood says it implemented the change on June 4, 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **SEC approval (April 2026).** The SEC approved FINRA's proposal to replace the day-trading margin provisions of Rule 4210 with an intraday margin standard. The amendments delete the "pattern day trader" definition, the related equity requirements and day-trading buying power calculations. — [WilmerHale client alert, 23 Apr 2026](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20260423-sec-approves-amendments-to-finra-rule-4210-replacing-day-trading-margin-requirements-with-a-modernized-intraday-margin-standard) (search snippet; page fetch blocked)
- **Approval date conflict.** Most sources give **April 14, 2026** ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/options/articles/sec-eliminates-25-000-pattern-050417843.html); [Benzinga, June 2026](https://cdn1.benzinga.com/trading-ideas/movers/26/06/53007723/robinhood-webull-interactive-brokers-set-to-gain-as-pdt-rule-dies-today)). One search summary gives April 15, 2026. The SEC order is Release No. 34-105226, File No. SR-FINRA-2025-017. — [SEC order PDF](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/finra/2026/34-105226.pdf)
- **Effective date and phase-in.** FINRA Regulatory Notice 26-10, published 20 April 2026, adopts "new intraday margin standards to replace the day trading margin requirements." The effective date is **June 4, 2026** and the phase-in for firms needing system upgrades ends **October 20, 2027**. — [FINRA Regulatory Notice 26-10](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/26-10); [Notice 26-10 PDF](https://www.finra.org/sites/default/files/2026-04/Regulatory-Notice-26-10.pdf); [ACA Group, "FINRA Ends the Pattern Day Trader Rule"](https://www.acaglobal.com/industry-insights/finra-ends-the-pattern-day-trader-rule/)
- **Follow-up guidance (May 2026).** FINRA Regulatory Notice 26-11 (19 May 2026) publishes interpretations of Rule 4210 and investor-education material for the new standard, also effective June 4, 2026. FINRA posts a separate "Interpretations of Rule 4210 (valid from June 4, 2026)." — [Regulatory Notice 26-11](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/26-11); [Interps of 4210 valid from June 4, 2026](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/guidance/interps-4210-202606)
- **How the intraday standard works.** Under new Rule 4210(d)(2), a firm must check each customer margin account for an "intraday margin deficit" on any day with an "IML-reducing transaction," meaning a trade that reduces the account's intraday margin level. The deficit is met if the customer makes net deposits, or otherwise raises the intraday margin level enough to cover it, by the end of a later day. — [King & Spalding, "FINRA Adopts Sweeping Changes to Margin Requirements for Day Trading"](https://www.kslaw.com/insights/articles/finra-adopts-sweeping-changes-to-margin-requirements-for-day-trading); [Orrick InfoBytes, 1 May 2026](https://infobytes.orrick.com/2026-05-01/finra-replaces-day-trading-margin-requirements-with-new-intraday-margin-standards/)
- **FINRA investor guidance.** The 25% maintenance standard now applies throughout the trading day rather than only at the close. A shortfall creates an intraday margin deficit that must be cured "as quickly as possible," and firms may impose stricter house requirements. — [FINRA, "Understanding the New Intraday Margin Requirements"](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/intraday-margin-requirements)
- **$2,000 floor kept.** FINRA's October 2026 blog says $2,000 remains the minimum for intraday leveraged trading and no equity minimum applies to day trading itself. The investor page adds that you can still trade with under $2,000 in an unleveraged account. — [FINRA blog, "An Update on FINRA Forward Rule Modernization" (12 Oct 2026)](https://www.finra.org/media-center/blog/an-update-on-finra-forward-rule-modernization-20261012); [FINRA investor insight](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/intraday-margin-requirements)
- **Cure-period conflict.** A summary of the proposal described the deficit as outstanding until satisfied or until a cutoff on the **15th business day** after it arose. I could not confirm that the final rule kept this number. ACA describes the obligation only as "as promptly as possible." — [Policy Risk summary of FR doc 2026-00519](https://policyrisk.com/federal-register/2026-00519); [ACA Group](https://www.acaglobal.com/industry-insights/finra-ends-the-pattern-day-trader-rule/)
- **Scope.** The PDT rule and its intraday successor cover US equities and equity options at FINRA member broker-dealers. Futures, forex and crypto are unaffected. — secondary: [Benzinga](https://cdn1.benzinga.com/trading-ideas/movers/26/06/53007723/robinhood-webull-interactive-brokers-set-to-gain-as-pdt-rule-dies-today); [TradeStation insights, 6 May 2026](https://www.tradestation.com/insights/2026/05/06/day-trade-rule-change/)
- **Robinhood implementation.** Robinhood's US support page says "On June 4, 2026, FINRA's new intraday margin standards will replace Pattern Day Trading (PDT)," and that PDT flags or restrictions on accounts will be removed. Secondary timelines list Robinhood, Webull, tastytrade, Fidelity and others as implementing on day one. — [Robinhood support, "Day trading"](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/day-trading/); [Benzinga](https://cdn1.benzinga.com/trading-ideas/movers/26/06/53007723/robinhood-webull-interactive-brokers-set-to-gain-as-pdt-rule-dies-today)
- **Old rule, for context.** Four or more day trades within five business days in a margin account triggered PDT status, which required $25,000 in equity. Several blogs misstate the trigger as "four trades per week." — [Wikipedia, Pattern day trader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pattern_day_trader); [Robinhood UK PDT page (legacy wording)](https://robinhood.com/gb/en/support/articles/pattern-day-trading)
- **Cash accounts were never PDT accounts.** They remain subject to T+1 settlement and good-faith-violation rules when trading with unsettled funds. — [tastylive, "Your biggest PDT rule questions answered before June 4th"](https://www.tastylive.com/news-insights/your-biggest-pdt-rule-questions-answered-before-june-4th); [Firstrade blog, 2026](https://blog.firstrade.com/blog/2026/the-complete-guide-to-the-pdt-rule-elimination-everything-investors-need-to-know-on-day-one)
- **Free-riding (Regulation T).** Paying for a purchase with the proceeds of selling the same securities is not permitted under the Federal Reserve Board's Regulation T, and the broker may freeze the account. — [SEC, "Freeriding"](https://www.sec.gov/answers/freeride.htm); [Investor.gov glossary](https://www.investor.gov/additional-resources/general-resources/glossary/freeriding)
- **Good-faith violation (GFV).** A GFV is selling a security bought in a cash account before it was paid for with settled funds. Fidelity's policy restricts an account to settled-cash trading after 3 GFVs, 3 cash-liquidation violations or 1 free-riding violation in 12 months. Interactive Brokers applies a 90-day "Cash Up Front" restriction after free-riding. These thresholds are **broker policy**, not a single FINRA rule, and they vary between brokers. — [Fidelity, Trading FAQs: Trading Restrictions](https://www.fidelity.com/trading/faqs-trading-restrictions); [IBKR knowledge base](https://ibkb.interactivebrokers.com/it/node/3101)

### Inferences
- An automated system no longer has to count day trades to avoid a PDT flag at US brokers that implemented on June 4, 2026, which includes Robinhood. In a margin account it must still avoid intraday margin deficits; in a cash account it must still respect settlement.
- Brokers may run the old PDT logic until October 20, 2027. A system that might connect to several brokers should read each broker's current status rather than assume PDT is gone everywhere.
- The intraday standard is computed continuously. A margin-account bot that builds large intraday exposure on a small balance can now hit a deficit mid-day, so the old Friday-to-Friday counting problem is replaced by a real-time exposure check.

### Gaps
- I could not open Notice 26-10, the SEC approval order or the final Rule 4210(d)(2) text, so the final cure period (whether the 15-business-day figure survived), any account restriction for an unmet deficit, and the exact IML calculation are unverified.
- The April 14 vs April 15, 2026 approval-date conflict is unresolved. The SEC order PDF (34-105226) would settle it.

## 2. Other rules that affect an automated retail trader (wash sales, short-term gains, T+1, margin, manipulation, MNPI, Reg SHO)

### Takeaway
Holding periods and trade frequency matter for tax as well as regulation. The wash-sale rule (a 61-day window that includes IRAs) can silently defer or permanently destroy losses for a bot that re-enters the same tickers. Short-term gains are taxed as ordinary income. Settlement has been T+1 since May 28, 2024. Spoofing, layering, pump-and-dump schemes and trading on material non-public information (MNPI) are fraud under Exchange Act §§9(a)(2) and 10(b) and Rule 10b-5, whether a human or an algorithm places the orders. Reg SHO (marking, locate, the Rule 201 price test, close-out) binds the broker but shapes which short orders a retail bot can place.

### Cited Findings
**Wash sales and tax**
- **Window.** A wash sale is a sale at a loss with a purchase of substantially identical securities within 30 days before or after the sale. Counting the sale day, the window is 61 days. — [TaxAct, quoting IRS Pub. 550](https://www.taxact.com/support/602); [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/investing/learn/wash-sale)
- **IRA purchases count.** Pub. 550 covers acquiring substantially identical stock "for your individual retirement arrangement (IRA) or Roth IRA." In a taxable account the disallowed loss is added to the basis of the replacement shares. An IRA purchase gets no basis adjustment, so the loss is effectively lost for good. Secondary sources say this also extends to a spouse's account. — [TaxAct](https://support.taxact.com/support/602/wash-sale-definition-and-entry); [Alpaca support, wash sale](https://alpaca.markets/support/wash-sale)
- **"Substantially identical" is not precisely defined.** Shares of different companies generally are not substantially identical. ETFs from different issuers tracking the same index are a grey area. — [ETF Trends](https://www.etftrends.com/fixed-income-channel/wash-sale-rules-bonds/); [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/investing/learn/wash-sale)
- **Section 475(f) mark-to-market.** A taxpayer who qualifies for trader tax status (a facts-and-circumstances test with no bright line) can elect §475(f). Covered trades then fall outside the wash-sale rule, gains and losses become ordinary, and the $3,000 annual capital-loss cap no longer applies to them. Trader status alone does not remove wash sales; the election does. Sources are commercial tax guides; one calculator site contradicts them and is likely wrong. — [Beancount.io, May 2026](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/05/11/trader-tax-status-section-475f-mark-to-market-election-wash-sale-rule-3000-capital-loss-cap-day-traders-guide); [E*TRADE, "Is trader status right for you?"](https://us.etrade.com/knowledge/library/taxes/talk-with-tax-advisor); [IRS written determination 202048009](https://irs.gov/pub/irs-wd/202048009.pdf)

**Settlement**
- **T+1.** The SEC adopted amendments to Rule 15c6-1 on 15 Feb 2023, moving most broker-dealer trades from T+2 to T+1. The compliance date was **May 28, 2024**, and FINRA aligned its rules to the same date. — [Davis Polk](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/sec-adopts-t-1-settlement-effective-may-2024); [White & Case](https://whitecase.com/insight-alert/sec-adopts-move-t1-standard-settlement-cycle); [A&O Shearman](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/t1-settlement-coming-may-28-2024)
- **Robinhood.** "Stocks and options take 1 trading day to settle." — [Robinhood, Settlement and buying power](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/settlement-and-buying-power/)

**Market manipulation**
- **Spoofing law.** Securities spoofing is charged under Exchange Act §§10(b) and 9(a)(2), Securities Act §17(a) and Rule 10b-5. The CFTC handles futures and commodities. Intent is pivotal: the regulator must show the trader intended to cancel when the order was placed. — [King & Spalding, "Spoofing: US Law and Enforcement"](https://www.kslaw.com/attachments/000/007/109/original/Spoofing_US_Law_and_Enforcement.pdf); [NexusFi explainer](https://nexusfi.com/a/regulation/spoofing-layering-futures-markets)
- **Recent individual case.** On 16 Dec 2025 the SEC filed settled charges against an individual trader for a two-year spoofing scheme that generated about $373,885 in gains, charged under §17(a) and §10(b). — [Morgan Lewis, Securities Enforcement Roundup, Dec 2025](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/01/securities-enforcement-roundup-december-2025)
- **FINRA rules cited for algorithmic strategies.** FINRA Rule 5210 addresses spoofing and manipulative strategies; Notice 15-09 also cites Rule 3110 (supervision). — [FINRA Regulatory Notice 15-09](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/15-09)
- **Social-media pump-and-dump (Dec 2022).** The SEC charged eight people (accounts vary between seven and eight) with a roughly $100M scheme. They touted stocks on Twitter and in Discord rooms, including "Atlas Trading" with about 150,000 members, then sold without disclosing their intent. DOJ filed parallel criminal charges. — [CNBC, 14 Dec 2022](https://www.cnbc.com/2022/12/14/sec-charges-social-media-influencers-in-alleged-100-million-fraud-scheme.html); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/sec-says-social-media-influencers-used-twitter-discord-manipulate-stoc-rcna61673); [KRDO/CNN](https://krdo.com/money/cnn-social-media-technology/2022/12/14/sec-charges-8-social-media-influencers-over-alleged-pump-and-dump-scheme/)

**Material non-public information**
- **Rule 10b5-1.** A trade is "on the basis of" MNPI if the trader was aware of the MNPI when trading. Other elements of insider trading, such as breach of duty, the classical and misappropriation theories, and tipping, come from case law under Rule 10b-5. The SEC amended Rule 10b5-1 in Dec 2022, adding cooling-off periods and certifications for 10b5-1 plans. — [Stoel Rives redline of Rule 10b5-1](https://files.stoel.com/files/ICYMI/Redline_10b5-1current_and_10b5-1new.pdf); [ABA Business Lawyer, 2024](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/business_law/resources/business-lawyer/2024-winter/the-myriad-ways-sec-rule-10b5-1-is-invalid); [Colonial Stock Transfer summary](https://blog.colonialstock.com/sec-adopts-amendments-to-rule-10b5-1-insider-trading-plans/)

**Reg SHO (short sales)**
- **Four parts.** Reg SHO has a marking requirement (Rule 200), a short-sale price-test circuit breaker (Rule 201, adopted 2010), a locate requirement (Rule 203(b)) and a close-out requirement (Rule 204). The locate requires the broker-dealer to locate borrowable securities before effecting a short sale. — [SEC Reg SHO FAQ](https://www.sec.gov/files/divisions/marketreg/mrfaqregsho1204.htm); [SEC 2004 adopting release](https://www.sec.gov/rules/2004/07/short-sales); [Nasdaq Trader Reg SHO page](https://m.nasdaqtrader.com/Trader.aspx?id=RegSho)
- **Rule 201 trigger.** Per study-guide sources (not primary), Rule 201 triggers on a decline of 10% or more from the prior close. While it is in effect, short sales are allowed only above the national best bid. — [CertFuel Series 24, Reg SHO](https://app.certfuel.com/series24/learn/trading-supervision/order-entry-routing-and-execution/regulation-sho); [Nasdaq Trader](https://m.nasdaqtrader.com/Trader.aspx?id=RegSho)

### Inferences
- **Wash-sale tracking is needed.** A breakout or news bot that repeatedly trades the same tickers will generate many wash sales. The system should track wash-sale-adjusted basis across all of the user's accounts, including IRAs, because brokers report wash sales only within one account and identical CUSIP.
- **Short-term gains tax.** Gains on positions held one year or less are taxed at ordinary income rates. This is a standard rule (IRS Topic No. 409), but I did not retrieve an IRS page in this session.
- **Cancel patterns can look like spoofing.** An automated system that submits and cancels many non-bona-fide limit orders could produce a spoofing-like pattern even without intent. Order and cancel logs should record the strategy reason for each cancel, as evidence of intent.
- **News trading is legal; MNPI is not.** Trading on news is lawful when the news is public. The risk arises if the "news" feed contains leaked or embargoed information, for example scraped pre-release content.
- **Reg SHO falls on the broker.** A retail bot trading through a broker does not perform locates itself. It must still handle broker rejections such as hard-to-borrow, Rule 201-restricted and no-shorting-in-cash-account.

### Gaps
- Under T+1, the exact Rule 204 close-out deadlines conflict in study-guide sources (open of T+2 vs T+4), and I did not verify them against the SEC's T+1 adopting release.
- The duration of a Rule 201 restriction (the rest of the trigger day plus the next day) was not confirmed against primary text.
- The §475(f) election deadline and procedure were not verified against the statute or IRS guidance.
- I could not confirm whether Robinhood currently permits stock short selling for retail accounts.

## 3. The $100, no-day-trading Robinhood case: cash vs Instant account, fractional shares, sizing and costs

### Takeaway
At $100, the account type barely matters for PDT. PDT no longer exists at Robinhood after June 4, 2026, and a $100 account cannot borrow on margin anyway because the $2,000 FINRA minimum applies. The real differences are settlement and deposits. A Robinhood **cash** account cannot trade with unsettled sale proceeds (T+1) and loses Instant Deposits, which largely prevents good-faith and free-riding violations. An **Instant** (margin-type) account can reuse unsettled proceeds immediately and has Instant Deposits. Fractional orders have a $1 minimum, cover NMS stocks only, are market orders in dollars during regular hours and limit-only in extended hours. With fixed-fractional sizing of 1–2% risk, each trade risks $1–$2. Any recurring paid cost dwarfs plausible returns: a $5 subscription, a $5 VPS, or a $199+ real-time data feed.

### Cited Findings
**Account types**
- **Margin vs cash at Robinhood.** Robinhood offers margin and cash accounts. In a margin account you can "instantly trade with funds from unsettled stock and option sales." In a cash account there is "No trading with unsettled funds from stock and options sales," and stocks and options take 1 trading day to settle. — [Robinhood, Settlement and buying power](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/settlement-and-buying-power/); [Robinhood investing accounts](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/robinhood-accounts/)
- **Instant is the margin-type default.** Per a secondary source, Robinhood's default for new users is the margin-type "Instant" account. Downgrading to cash removes Instant Deposit access and margin borrowing. Before June 2026, Instant accounts were subject to the PDT rule; cash accounts never were. — [Firstcard, "Robinhood Margin vs Cash Account"](https://www.firstcard.app/learn/robinhood-margin-account-vs-cash-account); [Finder, "Can You Day Trade on Robinhood?"](https://www.finder.com/stock-trading/robinhood-day-trading)
- **PDT gone at Robinhood (June 4, 2026).** PDT flags and restrictions are removed and the FINRA intraday margin standard replaces them. — [Robinhood support, Day trading](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/day-trading/)
- **No margin at $100.** $2,000 remains the minimum equity for leveraged or intraday leveraged trading, and accounts under $2,000 can still trade unleveraged. — [FINRA investor insight](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/intraday-margin-requirements); [FINRA blog, 12 Oct 2026](https://www.finra.org/media-center/blog/an-update-on-finra-forward-rule-modernization-20261012)
- **Instant Deposits.** They give buying power before the bank transfer settles; standard transfers can take up to 5 business days. The funds are provided "in good faith and are subject to limitations," and options trading may be restricted with them. — [Robinhood, Instant Deposits and Options](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/instant-deposits-and-options/); [Robinhood, When will my money be available?](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/when-will-my-money-be-available/)
- **Robinhood Gold.** Gold costs $5/month or $50/year. Gold accounts under $2,000 get Instant Deposit limits up to $5,000. Margin is optional, and not everyone is eligible. — [Robinhood, What's Robinhood Gold?](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/gold-overview/); [Robinhood, bigger Instant Deposits](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/bigger-instant-deposits/)
- **Violations still apply in cash accounts.** GFV and free-riding concepts still apply. The SEC notes that free-riding can lead a broker to freeze the account. — [SEC, Freeriding](https://www.sec.gov/answers/freeride.htm); [tastylive](https://www.tastylive.com/news-insights/your-biggest-pdt-rule-questions-answered-before-june-4th)

**Fractional shares**
- **$1 minimum.** Robinhood's Customer Agreement (revised **October 1, 2026**) says it will not accept dollar-based or share-based orders under $1.00. The support article adds that a full position worth under $1 can still be sold. — [Robinhood Customer Agreement (rev. 1 Oct 2026)](https://cdn.robinhood.com/assets/robinhood/legal/Robinhood-Customer-Agreement.pdf); [Robinhood, Fractional shares](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/fractional-shares/)
- **Eligibility.** Fractional trading covers NMS securities listed on exchanges such as Nasdaq and NYSE, not OTC stocks. An older support page says eligible stocks are generally priced above $1 with a market cap above $25M. The smallest unit is 1/1,000,000 share, with amounts rounded to the penny. Dollar orders are converted to shares at the prevailing price, so the actual notional can differ slightly. — [Robinhood, Fractional shares](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/fractional-shares/); [Robinhood Learn, investing with $1](https://robinhood.com/us/en/learn/articles/how-to-start-investing-for-as-little-as-1-dollar/); [Robinhood 2021 filing](https://investors.robinhood.com/static-files/85a7b27b-48e6-4d7d-9249-db02a839dd9b)
- **Order types by session.** In regular hours (9:30–16:00 ET), dollar-based and share-based buys default to market orders. In extended hours, buys are entered as limit orders with a preset limit price, because Robinhood's venues do not support market orders then. Not every security accepts fractional orders in extended hours; ineligible orders queue for the next regular open. Market sells are not allowed in extended or 24/5 sessions. — [Robinhood, Market order](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/market-order-update/); [Robinhood, Limit order](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/limit-order/); [Robinhood, Fractional shares](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/fractional-shares/)
- **Fractional shares cannot be transferred out of Robinhood.** — [BrokerChooser, Robinhood fractional shares](https://brokerchooser.com/invest-long-term/diversification/fractional-shares-robinhood)

**Sizing**
- **Fixed-fractional formula.** Shares = (Equity × Risk%) ÷ (Entry − Stop). 1% is the common default, and one source advises never exceeding 2% without exceptional reason. The formula assumes the stop fills at the stop price, so gaps can produce larger losses. — [TradeZella position size calculator](https://www.tradezella.com/blog/position-size-calculator); [Curved Trading, how to calculate position size](https://curvedtrading.com/articles/en/how/how-to-calculate-position-size/); [Adaptrade, fixed fractional](https://www.adaptrade.com/MSA/fixfrac.htm)

**Data and hosting costs**
- **Free vs paid data.** Alpaca's free tier provides real-time data from the IEX exchange only, a single venue, with delayed SIP data; full consolidated (SIP) data is paid. Example paid tiers: Marketstack Advanced at $199/month for real-time, and Intrinio Pro at $239/month billed annually or $299 monthly. Real-time consolidated redistribution to other users can cost five figures per month. — [APILayer, 12 best financial market APIs 2026](https://blog.apilayer.com/analyzing-the-top-free-apis-for-stock-data/); [Multiples.vc, Intrinio pricing 2026](https://multiples.vc/compare/intrinio-api-alternatives/md); [MarketData.app, stock market data licensing (Jan 2026)](https://www.marketdata.app/education/stocks/stock-market-data-licensing/)

### Inferences
**Recommended account type**
- For a $100, no-day-trade bot, a **cash account** is the safer default. It enforces "no trading with unsettled funds," which removes most GFV and free-riding risk at the broker level. Because the user forbids day trades, every position is held at least overnight. Under T+1, sale proceeds settle the next trading day, so capital can be recycled roughly daily without violations.
- The Instant account's benefits matter little at this size. Instant deposits and reuse of unsettled proceeds are of limited value, and margin cannot be used below $2,000.

**Day-trade guard**
- The bot should still have a hard "no same-day round trip" guard that blocks a sell of a lot bought the same session, since the user's no-day-trade rule is stricter than current law.
- The guard should cover a GTC stop that triggers the same day as the buy, which would itself create a day trade.
- If any broker the bot connects to is still on the old PDT logic before Oct 2027, the guard also protects that account.

**Sizing at $100** (illustrative)
- At 1–2% risk, each trade risks $1–$2. A $25 position with a 5% stop risks $1.25.
- With the $1 fractional minimum, a reasonable cap is 3–5 concurrent positions of $20–$33 each, which keeps some cash for the next day while proceeds settle.
- A daily loss limit of about 3% ($3) and a max drawdown kill at about 15–20% ($15–$20) would fit this scale.
- Exits on fractional positions during extended hours must be limit orders.
- Gaps and news spikes can blow through stops on small caps, so actual losses per trade may well exceed the planned $1–$2.

**Costs vs returns** (illustrative)
- Even a strong 20%/year on $100 is $20/year.
- A $5/month VPS ($60/year), Robinhood Gold ($50–$60/year) or any real-time SIP feed ($199+/month) exceeds that.
- Practical stack at this size: free data (Alpaca IEX, the broker's own quotes, delayed or EOD data for daily breakouts), a local machine or free-tier hosting, and paper trading as the main mode. Live trading is then a small validation step.
- IEX-only data reflects a single venue (a minority of volume). Breakout triggers and stale-data checks based on it may differ from the consolidated tape.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm whether Robinhood supports **stop or stop-limit orders on fractional or dollar-based positions** as of Oct 2026. If it does not, stops for fractional positions must be managed in software, which brings data-latency and gap risk. This should be verified in the app.
- Whether Robinhood currently offers an official API for equity orders, as opposed to its crypto API and unofficial libraries, is outside this brief and was not researched here. It determines whether automation is permissible under the Customer Agreement.
- Robinhood's own GFV counting and restriction policy for cash accounts was not found. Its cash account appears to block unsettled-fund trades outright rather than count violations, but this is unverified.
- The $25M market-cap fractional eligibility figure comes from an older support page.

## 4. Offering the app or connector to other people: what triggers registration?

### Takeaway
Trading your own account with your own tool does not by itself require SEC or FINRA registration. Registration risk comes from what you do for **others**:
- **Investment adviser.** Personalized or tailored signals for compensation trigger adviser status. Auto-execution tied to signals has defeated the publisher's exclusion, as in Weiss Research, 2006.
- **Broker-dealer.** Routing or handling other people's orders, especially for transaction-based compensation, triggers broker-dealer status; see Neovest, 2021.

The SEC's 2023 predictive-data-analytics conflicts proposal was **withdrawn in June 2025**. SEC exam priorities for FY2026 and FINRA's 2026 oversight report still focus heavily on AI, automated tools, AI agents and the accuracy of AI claims ("AI washing").

### Cited Findings
**Publisher's exclusion and signals**
- **Lowe v. SEC (1985).** The Supreme Court held that publications falling within the Advisers Act exclusion for "bona fide publications" did not make their publishers investment advisers. The case involved newsletters, not software. The Court noted no evidence of trading in recommended securities or of false content. — [Lowe v. SEC, 472 U.S. 181 (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/472/181); [Quimbee summary](https://quimbee.com/cases/lowe-v-sec)
- **Seeking Alpha (2024).** A 2024 S.D.N.Y. decision (Lingley v. Seeking Alpha) read the publisher's exclusion broadly for a financial-information website. — [Greenberg Traurig, "No need for Seeking Alpha to seek registration" (Aug 2024)](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2024/8/no-need-for-seeking-alpha-to-seek-registration); [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/judge-dismisses-case-against-seeking-alpha-implications-publishers-financial)
- **Auto-trading arrangements.** Practitioners warn that operators of auto-trading platforms and quantitative or model-portfolio providers must structure carefully to keep the exclusion. The SEC's 2006 Weiss Research order sanctioned a newsletter publisher as an unregistered adviser in connection with auto-trading arrangements. — [Interactive Brokers webinar, "Spotlight: Publisher Exclusion"](https://brokerage.ibkr.com/webinars/spotlight-publisher-exclusion.pdf); [Cahill memo, In the Matter of Weiss Research](https://www.cahill.com/publications/firm-memoranda/0000001/_res/id=Attachments/index=0/CGR%20Firm%20Memo%20-%20In%20the%20Matter%20of%20Weiss%20Research%20Financial%20Newsletter%20Publisher%20Sanctioned%20as%20an%20Unregistered%20Investment%20Adviser.pdf)
- **Subscription trading-site operators.** The SEC has treated subscription day-trading site operators as investment advisers based on their conduct (DynamicDaytrader.com, 2000). It has charged unregistered advisers who sent stock promotions to paying subscribers while trading the stock themselves (Banister / Market Analysts Group). In 2025 it pursued schemes that promoted purported "AI-generated trading signals" in WhatsApp groups (Cryptoaiml), citing Advisers Act antifraud provisions. — [SEC Lit. Rel. 16475 (DynamicDaytrader)](https://www.sec.gov/litigation/litreleases/lr-16475); [SEC Lit. Rel. 26187](https://www.sec.gov/enforcement-litigation/litigation-releases/lr-26187); [Edgex news on Cryptoaiml](https://pro.edgex.exchange/en-US/news/article/sec-charges-cryptoaiml-12-5-million-crypto-fraud)

**Internet adviser exemption**
- **2024 amendments.** The SEC amended the exemption on 27 Mar 2024. The amendments were effective 8 Jul 2024 with a **compliance date of 31 Mar 2025**. They removed the de minimis allowance for fewer than 15 non-internet clients and require all advice to be delivered exclusively through an "operational interactive website," with a Form ADV Schedule D representation. Advisers no longer eligible had to register with states and withdraw from SEC registration; one source gives 29 Jun 2025. — [Goodwin, Apr 2024](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2024/04/alerts-finance-pif-the-sec-amends-the-internet-adviser-exemption); [K&L Gates, 15 Apr 2024](https://www.klgates.com/The-SEC-Limits-the-Internet-Adviser-Exemption-4-15-2024); [Stradley Ronon](https://www.stradley.com/publications/narrowing-the-gap-the-sec-limits-advisers-internet-exemption)

**Broker-dealer registration**
- **Neovest (2021).** The SEC's first case charging an order-management/execution-system (OEMS) provider as an unregistered broker-dealer. Neovest paid $2.75M for routing customer orders while receiving transaction-based compensation. Commissioner Peirce dissented that transaction-based compensation "has all but swallowed the broker analysis." — [SEC press release 2021-113](https://www.sec.gov/news/press-release/2021-113); [Finadium](https://finadium.com/sec-charges-neovest-2-75mn-for-operating-as-an-unregistered-broker-dealer/); [National Law Review analysis](https://natlawreview.com/node/153349/printable/print)
- **April 2026 SEC staff statement (crypto only).** For crypto-asset securities user interfaces, the staff described conditions for operating without broker-dealer registration: only a "fixed charge" that is objective and agnostic to product, route, venue and counterparty; no payment for order flow; no negotiation, solicitation or holding out as a broker. It is limited to Exchange Act §15(a) and to crypto-asset securities. — [WilmerHale, 17 Apr 2026](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20260417-sec-staff-issues-broker-dealer-registration-guidance-for-certain-user-interfaces); [Fintech & Digital Assets blog, Apr 2026](https://www.fintechanddigitalassets.com/2026/04/sec-staff-issues-statement-on-broker-dealer-registration-for-cryptoasset-securities-interfaces/)
- **Broker onboarding questions.** Interactive Brokers' Web API onboarding questionnaire asks third-party apps how orders reach IBKR and what compensation they receive. — [IBKR Web API onboarding questionnaire](https://us2.interactivebrokers.com/download/WebAPI_Onboarding_Questionnaire.pdf)

**SEC actions on AI and predictive data analytics (2023–2026)**
- **Proposal withdrawn.** The July 2023 proposal would have required broker-dealers and advisers to "eliminate or neutralize" conflicts from predictive data analytics. It was **formally withdrawn on 12 June 2025**; the SEC rule page lists the withdrawal as effective 17 June 2025. It was one of 14 Gensler-era proposals withdrawn. The SEC said any future action would come as a new proposal. — [Paul Hastings](https://www.paulhastings.com/thought-leadership/sec-withdraws-14-pending-rule-proposals); [SEC rule page S7-12-23](https://www.sec.gov/rules/2023/07/s7-12-23); [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/sec-formally-withdraws-fourteen-rule-proposals)
- **AI-washing cases (2024).** In 2024 the SEC settled AI-washing charges against advisers Delphia and Global Predictions under the Marketing Rule. It also brought an Oct 2024 settled action against an adviser that marketed an AI-driven trading platform it had not implemented. — [Thomson Reuters Institute](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en/institute/articles/ai-washing-enforcement); [Winston & Strawn](https://www.winston.com/en/blogs-and-podcasts/capital-markets-and-securities-law-watch/sec-targets-ai-washing-by-companies-investment-advisers-and-broker-dealers)
- **Under Chairman Atkins.** As of Nov 2025, the Atkins-led SEC had not brought an adviser AI-washing case. It did sue six advisers on 13 Nov 2025 over unsubstantiated Form ADV statements. A Cyber and Emerging Technologies Unit was created in Enforcement in Feb 2025; one secondary source says its mandate covers AI-driven manipulation and algorithmic trading failures. — [Gibson Dunn SEC Sentinel](https://secsentinel.gibsondunn.com/what-investment-advisers-should-expect-from-sec-enforcement-post-shutdown/); [ai2.work blog (secondary, unverified)](https://ai2.work/blog/sec-ai-exam-sweep-targets-ai-washing-and-trading-model-claims)
- **FY2026 exam priorities.** Released 17 Nov 2025. Examiners will review registrants' use of AI, automated investment tools, and trading algorithms and platforms. That includes whether automated advice fits each investor's profile, whether AI claims are accurate, and whether governance and human oversight exist. — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/-/media/files/perspectives-events/publications/2025/11/legal-update---sec-2026-exam-priorities.pdf%3Frev=52faa27aee1a4cdd8e3da29a8ea3b18c); [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/articles/2025/sec-2026-priorities-examinations-and-perspectives-reg-alert.html); [Katten](https://katten.com/sec-division-of-examinations-releases-its-2026-examination-priorities); [Sia Partners](https://www.sia-partners.com/en/insights/publications/sec-division-examinations-releases-2026-priorities)

**FINRA guidance on AI and algorithmic trading**
- **Regulatory Notice 24-09 (27 June 2024).** FINRA rules and securities laws apply to firms' use of generative AI and LLMs, including AI embedded in third-party tools. The rules are "technology neutral" and the notice creates no new requirements. It urges model inventories, pre-deployment testing and output checks, and Rule 2210 applies to AI-generated communications. — [FINRA Regulatory Notice 24-09](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/24-09); [Mayer Brown summary](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2024/07/finra-reminds-members-of-regulatory-obligations-when-using-generative-artificial-intelligence-ai-and-large-language-models)
- **2026 Annual Regulatory Oversight Report (9 Dec 2025).** It adds **AI agents** as a focus and flags autonomy (acting without human approval) and scope creep (exceeding intended authority). It suggests agent-specific supervision, tracking agent actions and restricting agents' system access. — [Debevoise, Dec 2025](https://www.debevoise.com/insights/publications/2025/12/finras-2026-regulatory-oversight-report-continued); [Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/zh-hans/insights/newsupdates/2025/12/finra-issues-2026-regulatory-oversight-report); [FINRA press release via BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251209886837/en)
- **Regulatory Notice 15-09 (Mar 2015).** Effective practices for algorithmic strategies fall into five areas: general risk assessment and response; software and code development and implementation; software testing and system validation; trading systems; compliance. Examples include testing before production and piloting new strategies at limited size, scaling up only as results are confirmed. — [FINRA Regulatory Notice 15-09](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/15-09); [Skadden summary](https://www.skadden.com/-/media/files/publications/2015/05/finraprovidesguidanceoneffectivesupervisionandcont.pdf)
- **Algo-developer registration (2016).** FINRA requires registration as Securities Traders for associated persons who design or significantly modify algorithmic trading strategies at member firms. This applies to broker-dealer staff, not to retail users. — [Hedge Fund Journal](https://thehedgefundjournal.com/new-finra-registration-requirement-for-algo-traders/); [Venable, 13 Sept 2016](https://www.venable.com/New-Requirements-for-Algorithmic-Traders-May-Prove-Challenging-09-13-2016)

### Inferences
**Safest postures for a solo developer, from low to high risk**
1. Personal use only: no registration.
2. Open-source code that users self-host with their own broker keys and their own strategies, with no compensation tied to trades and no personalized advice: low risk. This is closer to selling a tool than to advising or brokering.
3. A paid, impersonal, regularly published signal or newsletter with no auto-execution: arguably within the publisher's exclusion, but fact-specific.
4. Signals that auto-execute in subscribers' accounts, personalized recommendations, or fees per trade or tied to order flow: likely adviser and/or broker-dealer registration.

**Other points**
- Advisers with under about $100M in regulatory AUM generally register with states, not the SEC. This is general knowledge and was not verified in this session.
- The withdrawal of the predictive-data-analytics rule does not remove existing fiduciary and Reg BI duties, or antifraud liability for overstating AI capabilities. A public app should not claim "AI-powered" performance it cannot substantiate.
- FINRA's AI-agent concerns about autonomy and scope creep map directly onto an MCP trading connector. Gating every order behind explicit human approval, logging agent actions and limiting tool scope is consistent with regulator expectations, even though FINRA's rules bind member firms rather than individual users.

- I did not retrieve the Weiss Research order itself or the Lowe opinion text in full, so the exact three-part publisher's-exclusion test (impersonal, bona fide, general and regular circulation) is from background knowledge rather than a fetched page.
- There is no SEC guidance specific to equity order-routing APIs or retail "connectors" (the April 2026 statement is crypto-only).
- No 2026 SEC enforcement action involving AI in a retail brokerage app was found.
- State registration thresholds and state "investment adviser representative" rules were not researched.

## 5. Standard risk controls for automated trading (lessons from Knight Capital, Citigroup and others)

### Takeaway
The standard controls are pre-trade hard blocks: max notional per order, max position, price collars against the reference price (fat-finger checks), order-rate limits, duplicate and idempotency checks, and kill switches with "halted" and "reduce-only" states. They are backed by alerts that are designed to be read, staged rollouts, removal of dead code, and paper trading before live. Knight (2012) and Citi (2022) both failed on missing hard blocks and alerting that people could ignore or override.

### Cited Findings
- **Knight Capital (1 Aug 2012).** A faulty deployment left an unused function active in the order router. It sent 4 million+ erroneous orders in about 45 minutes, traded 397 million+ shares and lost $460M+. Before the open the system had sent 97 automated emails flagging an error; nobody acted because the emails were not designed as alerts. The SEC cited inadequate pre-trade controls, such as no check comparing orders leaving the router against orders entered, and exposure controls that relied on human monitoring rather than automated alerts. Knight paid a **$12M penalty** in Oct 2013, the first enforcement under Market Access Rule 15c3-5. — [SEC press release 2013-222](https://www.sec.gov/news/press-release/2013-222); [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/knight-capital-settles-rule-15c3-5-violations-with-sec-agrees-to-pay-12-million); [Seward & Kissel](https://brokerdealings.sewkis.com/sec-fines-knight-capital-12-million-in-first-ever-market-access-rule-enforcement-action/?print=pdf)
- **Citigroup "fat finger" (2 May 2022).** A trader entered a basket sale valued at **$444bn instead of $58m**. Controls blocked $255bn, $189bn reached an execution algorithm, and about $1.4bn sold before cancellation. The FCA found "no hard block" that would reject the whole erroneous basket. Poor design also let the trader override a pop-up alert without scrolling through all warnings. The FCA fined £27.8m and the PRA £33.9m (total £61.6m, about $79m) on 22 May 2024. — [MarketsMedia](https://www.marketsmedia.com/citi-fined-61-6m-for-fat-finger-trade/); [GRC Report](https://grcreport.com/post/fca-slaps-citigroup-with-ps27-7-million-fine-over-faulty-trading-controls); [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2024/05/22/citigroup-fined-millions-in-uk-over-fat-finger-trading-error)
- **FINRA 15-09 practices.** Pre-production testing, staged pilot deployment at limited size, code-change controls and compliance involvement. — [FINRA Regulatory Notice 15-09](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/15-09)
- **NautilusTrader pre-trade risk engine.** Trading states: ACTIVE; **REDUCING**, which only allows orders that reduce open positions; and **HALTED**, which denies all commands except cancels. It enforces `max_notional_per_order` per instrument and a `max_order_submit_rate` (limit per time interval). Denied orders raise an `OrderDenied` event and never reach the venue, with reason codes such as NOTIONAL_EXCEEDS_MAX_PER_ORDER, RATE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED, TRADING_HALTED and TRADING_STATE_REDUCING. — [NautilusTrader risk API (Python)](https://nautilustrader.io/docs/python-api-latest/risk.html); [NautilusTrader execution concepts](https://nautilustrader.io/docs/nightly/concepts/execution); [Rust RiskEngine docs](https://nautilustrader.io/docs/rust-api-latest/nautilus_risk/engine/struct.RiskEngine.html)
- **Freqtrade "protections" (crypto bot).** StoplossGuard halts trading after N stop-loss exits in a lookback window. MaxDrawdown locks trading when drawdown over recent trades exceeds a threshold. CooldownPeriod blocks immediate re-entry into a pair after an exit. Protections can be enabled in backtests with `--enable-protections`. Protections were labelled Beta in the 2024.1 docs, and config-file protections are deprecated in favour of strategy-defined ones. — [Freqtrade protections docs](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/latest/includes/protections/); [Freqtrade 2024.1 protections](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/2024.1/includes/protections)
- **Limit Up-Limit Down (LULD).** For stocks above $3, Tier 1 (S&P 500, Russell 1000, some ETPs) has 5% bands and Tier 2 (other NMS stocks) has 10% bands; stocks at $3 or below get wider bands. If a stock stays at its band (a "limit state") for 15 seconds, a 5-minute trading pause follows, which can be extended. Bands double near the open and close; sources conflict on whether the opening window is the first 15 or 25 minutes, while the closing window is the last 25 minutes. — [FINRA, Guardrails for market volatility](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/guardrails-market-volatility); [Investor.gov, measures to address market volatility](https://www.investor.gov/news-alerts/investor-bulletins/measures-address-market-volatility); [Nasdaq LULD FAQ](https://m.nasdaqtrader.com/content/MarketRegulation/LULD_FAQ.pdf); [LULD Plan site](https://www.luldplan.com)

### Inferences
**Controls checklist for a retail system** (synthesised from the cases and frameworks above)
1. **Max position size** as % of equity and as absolute dollars, plus max concurrent positions. At $100, see section 3.
2. **Daily loss limit.** On breach, switch to REDUCING or HALTED until the next session, and require a manual reset.
3. **Kill switch.** One command or button that cancels all open orders and sets HALTED. Optionally flatten positions. It must be independent of the strategy process.
4. **Order-rate limits** per minute and per day, plus a max-orders-per-symbol-per-day cap. This protects against Knight-style loops and spoofing-like patterns.
5. **Fat-finger checks.** Reject orders whose notional exceeds a cap, whose quantity exceeds N× typical size, or whose limit price is more than X% from the last trade or NBBO. These must be hard blocks, not dismissible warnings (the Citi lesson).
6. **Duplicate-order protection.** A deterministic client order ID derived from the signal (strategy, symbol, signal timestamp), a check for existing open orders and positions before submitting, idempotent retries after timeouts, and reconciliation with the broker before resubmitting.
7. **Halt and LULD handling.** Detect halt and pause status. Do not queue market orders into a reopening auction. Widen or suspend breakout triggers near bands. Treat the first 15 minutes after open and after a halt as special regimes.
8. **Stale-data detection.** Track the last quote or trade timestamp per symbol and the feed heartbeat. Block new entries if data is older than a threshold, or if the feed's timestamp is far from the wall clock.
9. **Paper trading before live, then a small live pilot** (FINRA 15-09's limited-size pilot).
10. **Deployment hygiene.** Remove dead code and flags, run identical code across all instances, add pre-open self-tests, and route alerts to a channel that pages a human (the Knight lesson).

### Gaps
- No primary source was retrieved for retail-broker-specific risk limits, for example Robinhood's own collars on market orders or its order-rate limits.
- Other failures, such as the 2010 Flash Crash and the 2013 Goldman options error, were not researched in this session.

## 6. Reference architecture for a small retail system (frameworks, storage, scheduling, secrets, audit, monitoring, MCP gating)

### Takeaway
The architecture is an event-driven pipeline: market and news data feed → normalizer and stale-data guard → strategy, producing signals → risk engine with pre-trade hard checks → human approval gate → order manager with idempotent client IDs → broker adapter → fills, reconciliation and an audit log. It should share one strategy codebase between backtest and live. NautilusTrader and LEAN are built for this; backtrader can do it with more plumbing; vectorbt is research-only; Freqtrade is crypto-only. An AI-assistant (MCP) connector should never place orders directly. It should create a pending order "proposal" that a human confirms out-of-band, using a server-enforced confirmation token or a separate confirmer process holding the trade key. MCP tool annotations and client prompts are only hints.

### Cited Findings
**Frameworks**
- **NautilusTrader.** Backtesting and live trading run on the same engine and message bus; only the data source and order destination differ. Strategy code is reused across backtest and live, but you source your own data and operate the environment. — [MarketCalls, Introduction to NautilusTrader](https://www.marketcalls.in/python/introduction-to-nautilus-trader-part-1-architecture-backtesting-openalgo.html); [ForexTester, backtrader alternatives](https://forextester.com/blog/backtrader-alternatives/)
- **QuantConnect LEAN.** An open-source engine. The LEAN CLI runs strategies in Docker with the same packages as QuantConnect.com and supports local backtesting, optimization and **local live trading**. Brokerage options include Tradier (demo or real). — [LEAN CLI on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/lean/1.0.99/); [QuantConnect CLI page](https://quantconnect.com/cli); [LEAN CLI workflows docs](https://quantconnect.com/docs/v2/lean-cli/projects/workflows)
- **Backtrader.** Event-driven, with live integrations for IB, OANDA and Alpaca that need extra coding. Its IB guide still points to the old IbPy package. — [ForexTester](https://forextester.com/blog/backtrader-alternatives/); [TradingBrokers, Backtrader vs vectorbt](https://tradingbrokers.com/backtrader-vs-vectorbt/)
- **vectorbt.** Vectorized NumPy/Numba code for testing thousands of parameter combinations quickly, at the cost of detailed execution modelling. It is not an event-driven live engine. — [TradingBrokers](https://tradingbrokers.com/backtrader-vs-vectorbt/)
- **Freqtrade.** A crypto bot with dry-run, backtesting and built-in protections. — [Freqtrade protections docs](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/latest/includes/protections/)

**MCP connector design**
- **Spec language.** The MCP spec (2024-11-05 and 2025-03-26 revisions) says "there SHOULD always be a human in the loop with the ability to deny tool invocations." Apps should show which tools are exposed and when they are invoked, and clients SHOULD "prompt for user confirmation on sensitive operations." The protocol does not mandate a specific interaction model. — [MCP spec 2025-03-26, Tools](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2025-03-26/server/tools); [MCP spec 2025-06-18, Tools](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2025-06-18/server/tools)
- **Annotations are hints.** Tool annotations (readOnlyHint, destructiveHint, idempotentHint, openWorldHint) let hosts decide which calls need confirmation, but the host decides. Elicitation lets a server ask the user for confirmation mid-call, but only if the client implements it. — [PromptLayer, MCP elicitation](https://www.promptlayer.com/glossary/mcp-elicitation); [Glama, epicweb elicitation exercise](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/@epicweb-dev/advanced-mcp-features/blob/0dc369ab4c3a427a0f4691973062e1f2f6875b65/exercises/02.elicitation/README.mdx)
- **tastytrade's official MCP server.** It uses a dry-run-first flow and requires a **confirmation token** for the five tools that submit or change orders. All tools carry MCP annotations. — [tastytrade developer docs, MCP server](https://developer.tastytrade.com/docs/sdks-and-tools/mcp-server/)
- **kainext-binance-mcp.** It splits into two processes: the model-facing server holds only a **read-only key**, and a separate **confirmer** holds the trade key and shows a native OS dialog whose default button is Cancel. Another server supports a Telegram approval mode (`MCP_ORDER_APPROVAL_MODE=telegram`). — [Socket.dev, kainext-binance-mcp](https://socket.dev/pypi/package/kainext-binance-mcp); [Glama, trade-mcp](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/Aaditya2502/trade-mcp)
- **FINRA on agents.** FINRA's 2026 report recommends tracking AI-agent actions and restricting agents' system access. — [Debevoise](https://www.debevoise.com/insights/publications/2025/12/finras-2026-regulatory-oversight-report-continued)

### Inferences
**Reference design** (synthesis; not from a single source)
- **Event bus and components.** Async in-process for a solo developer, for example Python asyncio or NautilusTrader's bus. Components:
  - DataFeed: websocket quotes and bars, plus news.
  - Normalizer and StaleGuard.
  - Strategy: a pure function of events to signals, shared by backtest and live.
  - RiskEngine: hard checks from section 5.
  - ApprovalGate.
  - OMS: client-order-ID generation, state machine, retries.
  - BrokerAdapter.
  - Reconciler: on startup and every N minutes, compares broker positions and orders with local state and halts on mismatch.
- **Backtest/live parity.** The backtester feeds historical events through the *same* Strategy and RiskEngine code with a simulated broker adapter, so only the adapters differ.
- **Storage.**
  - Raw market data: Parquet files partitioned by date and symbol, or DuckDB, for research.
  - Orders, fills, approvals and positions: SQLite or Postgres, transactional.
  - Audit log: append-only JSONL or a database table with hash-chaining, recording every signal, risk decision, approval (who, when, what was shown), order, broker acknowledgement and fill.
  - Point-in-time universe and corporate-action tables to avoid survivorship and look-ahead bias (section 7).
- **Scheduling.** Use an exchange-calendar-aware scheduler, for example cron or APScheduler plus a market-calendar library, for pre-open self-tests, the open and close, end-of-day reconciliation and wash-sale and tax reports. Respect early closes and holidays.
- **Secrets.** Broker credentials go in an OS keychain or secret manager, never in the repo or the LLM context. Use separate read-only and trading credentials where the broker allows. The MCP server process that the model talks to should hold only read-only scope; order execution lives in a separate process (the kainext pattern). Rotate tokens and use least privilege.
- **Monitoring and alerts.** Feed heartbeat, stale-data alarms, order rejects, P&L versus daily loss limit, reconciliation mismatches and process liveness, sent as push or Telegram alerts that are designed to be read. Knight's 97 ignored emails show why.
- **MCP order gating pattern.**
  1. `propose_order` (read-only effect) validates against the RiskEngine and returns a human-readable preview: symbol, side, quantity or dollars, order type and limit, estimated cost, risk-to-stop, and why the strategy fired. It also returns a single-use, short-TTL confirmation token bound to those exact parameters.
  2. The human confirms **outside the model's control**: a native dialog, phone push or web UI, not a chat message the model could fabricate.
  3. `submit_order(token)` is accepted only with a valid, unexpired, unused token whose parameters match.
  4. The server re-runs risk checks at submit time and logs the full chain.
  5. Mark order tools `destructiveHint: true` / `readOnlyHint: false`, but do not rely on client behaviour.
  6. Never expose a "submit without token" tool or a bulk or loop tool. Rate-limit proposals.

## 7. Backtesting pitfalls (survivorship, look-ahead, overfitting and multiple testing, walk-forward, small-cap slippage)

### Takeaway
Most retail backtests are biased upward by:
- testing today's tickers (survivorship bias)
- using revised or not-yet-public data (look-ahead bias)
- trying many variants and reporting the best one (multiple testing)
- assuming mid-price fills on small caps (slippage)

The remedies are point-in-time universes including delisted names, lagged inputs, a held-out set that is never touched, walk-forward validation, the Deflated Sharpe Ratio or probability of backtest overfitting to discount for the number of trials, and costs of at least the full spread plus impact. For breakout and news trading in small caps, slippage assumptions can decide whether a strategy is profitable at all.

### Cited Findings
- **Survivorship bias.** Today's constituent list is the wrong universe for historical tests; one source says roughly a third to a half of the S&P 500 turns over every ten years. Use datasets that keep dead tickers and delisted securities with their final returns. — [LuxAlgo, survivorship bias](https://www.luxalgo.com/blog/survivorship-bias-in-backtesting-explained); [DEV Community, survivorship vs look-ahead](https://dev.to/tradevodata/survivorship-bias-vs-lookahead-bias-the-two-silent-backtest-killers-pmm)
- **Look-ahead bias.** One source calls it the most common backtesting mistake; revised fundamentals overwriting originally published values are a typical cause. A practical test is to lag every input one more period and check whether the edge survives. — [AnalystPrep, CFA L2 Problems in Backtesting](https://analystprep.com/study-notes/cfa-level-2/problems-in-backtesting/); [DEV Community](https://dev.to/tradevodata/survivorship-bias-vs-lookahead-bias-the-two-silent-backtest-killers-pmm)
- **Deflated Sharpe Ratio (Bailey & López de Prado, J. Portfolio Mgmt, 2014).** It corrects the Sharpe ratio for selection bias under multiple testing and for non-normal returns. Inputs: observed Sharpe, number of trials, variance of the trials' Sharpes, skewness, kurtosis and sample length. It outputs a deflated value and a p-value. Testing more variants raises the expected maximum (luckiest) Sharpe. — [SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551); [R quantstrat deflatedSharpe docs](https://www.rdocumentation.org/packages/quantstrat/versions/0.16.7/topics/deflatedSharpe); [CXO Advisory](https://cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/sharper-sharpe-ratio)
- **Overfitting examples.** In one setup, Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado et al. show no more than **45 variations** should be tried before the result is overfit; this is specific to that example. In another, the optimal in-sample Sharpe of **1.59** became **−0.18** out of sample. — [Bailey et al., overfitting tools PDF](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/overfit-tools-at.pdf); [SSRN 2507040, "Statistical Overfitting and Backtest Performance"](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2507040)
- **Hold-out data.** Reserve data you genuinely never look at until final validation. — [AnalystPrep](https://analystprep.com/study-notes/cfa-level-2/problems-in-backtesting/)
- **Slippage on small caps.**
  - Round-trip cost cannot be below the bid-ask spread. A $2 stock with a $0.02 spread implies a 100 bps minimum before impact. One guide suggests slippage = max(half-spread, square-root impact).
  - Square-root impact scales with √(order size / ADV) times volatility; one guide applies it once trades exceed about 5% of ADV.
  - Rule-of-thumb small-cap costs: 15–50 bps; 25–100 bps for $1–10 stocks; 50–500 bps for microcaps and pre-market. These are uncalibrated defaults. — [skills.cat transaction-cost modelling](https://skills.cat/skills/jefrnc/quant-llm-skills/transaction-cost-modeling); [LuxAlgo, slippage and liquidity](https://luxalgo.com/blog/backtesting-limitations-slippage-and-liquidity-explained); [qf-lib SquareRootMarketImpactSlippage](https://qf-lib.readthedocs.io/en/stable/_autosummary/qf_lib.backtesting.execution_handler.slippage.square_root_market_impact_slippage.SquareRootMarketImpactSlippage.html); [rustybt slippage docs](https://rustybt.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api/order-management/transaction-costs/slippage/)

### Inferences
- **Fills for breakout and news strategies.** Model entries at the *ask* (or ask plus ticks) at the bar after the signal, never the signal bar's close. Model stops as filling at the next available price after a gap, not at the stop level. Exclude or haircut fills during LULD pauses and the first minutes after halts.
- **$100 account.** Market impact is negligible at this size, but spread cost is not. Fractional market orders on $1–$5 stocks with wide spreads can cost 1–3% round trip, which overwhelms typical breakout edges. Prefer liquid, tight-spread names or limit entries.
- **Walk-forward protocol.** Optimise on window *k* and test on window *k+1*, roll forward, and report only the concatenated out-of-sample results. Count every configuration tried and feed that count into the DSR.
- **Paper trading as a check.** Paper trading tests execution assumptions as well as signals. Compare paper fills with the backtest's assumed fills before going live.
