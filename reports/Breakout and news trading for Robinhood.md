# Breakout Edges Vanish When Hindsight Disappears

A $100 account that cannot day trade should not expect breakout or news trading to beat an index fund, so the first thing to build is a way to measure results, not an autonomous bot. In this project's 2005–2026 backtests, the classic triggers added nothing once survivorship drift was removed. Those triggers were new 52-week highs, volume-confirmed highs and volatility-squeeze breakouts. Every small-cap breakout rule lost money after costs, and a Donchian trend portfolio compounded below SPY. News catalysts deliver their big moves on the announcement day or overnight, before a next-morning order can fill: 15–35% for takeover targets, and most of the reaction to earnings and FDA news. Only one signal kept a defensible residual edge: a large-cap gap-up of at least 4% on at least 3× normal volume, bought at the next open and held one to three months. It beat the equal-weight universe by roughly 0.4–0.8% with a t-statistic near 1.9, was concentrated in a handful of stocks picked with hindsight, and was not statistically significant in 2016–2026 on its own. The IREN marketing post fails the same scrutiny. One fixed trend rule turned IREN's +616% into +142%, and it lost money on 7 of 11 crypto-miner and AI-hosting peers (median −19%, against +159% for buy-and-hold). Robinhood's official Trading MCP is the right channel because it confines trading to a separately funded Agentic Account. However, approvals are off by default for external agents, the agent can read all of your Robinhood accounts, and whether it accepts fractional orders is unverified. Build a nightly scanner and trade journal that drafts order tickets for that single gap-on-volume signal: a human approves every trade, positions are five $20 slots, exits are time-based, and every signal is paper-tracked against SPY. At roughly 20 live trades a year, the $100 account by itself would need decades to prove an edge.

## Only slow momentum in liquid stocks survives costs and hindsight

### The research favors slow signals held overnight

The strongest long-sample evidence supports breakouts measured slowly and held for weeks to months. Examples are nearness to the 52-week high, 12-month momentum and all-time-high trend following. **Stocks near their 52-week high outperform those far from it**, and that nearness explains much of momentum's profit ([George & Hwang via Marquette](https://epublications.marquette.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1168&context=fin_fac)). Barroso and Wang dispute this, arguing that the effect is limited to small stocks and is really ordinary momentum (same source). The no-day-trading constraint costs this family of signals almost nothing, because **momentum's abnormal return is earned almost entirely overnight, while the intraday component is insignificant** ([Lou, Polk & Skouras, JFE 2019](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/87481)). At horizons of one week to one month, the usual pattern is reversal: last month's winners lag. The exception is high-turnover large caps, where **short-term momentum survives transaction costs and is strongest in the largest, most liquid, most-covered stocks** ([Medhat & Schmeling, RFS 2022](https://repec.cepr.org/repec/cpr/ceprdp/DP15857.pdf)).

These edges are thin and fragile. Among 7,846 technical rules tested on the Dow, **transaction costs fully offset even the in-sample performance of the best rules, and the outperformance vanished in 1997–2007** ([Bajgrowicz & Scaillet, JFE 2012](https://ideas.repec.org:443/a/eee/jfinec/v106y2012i3p473-491.html)). Published anomalies lose about **58% of their performance after publication** ([McLean & Pontiff via arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.01380)). The Bollinger-band squeeze was unprofitable even before costs after 2001 ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/when-bollinger-bands-snapped/)). Momentum also crashes: in March–May 2009, past losers rose about 163% while past winners rose 8% ([Daniel & Moskowitz, NBER](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w20439/w20439.pdf)). Where trend following still works on stocks, **under 7% of trades generate most of the profit** ([Zarattini, Pagani & Wilcox via CXO](https://cxoadvisory.com/momentum-investing/all-time-high-trend-following)), and win rates sit near 49% ([Wilcox & Crittenden](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~mkearns/finread/trend.pdf)). Chart-pattern failure rates rose from 11% of upward breakouts in 1991 to 44% in 2007 ([Bulkowski, Stocks & Commodities](https://www.traders.com/Documentation/FEEDbk_docs/2009/05/Bulkowski.html)).

For spotting a breakout early, abnormal volume has better support than any chart pattern. The high-volume return premium predicts higher returns for about 50 days, because a volume shock raises a firm's visibility ([Gervais, Kaniel & Mingelgrin, NYU copy](https://archive.nyu.edu/bitstream/2451/27578/2/SSRN-id1122463.pdf)). Peer-reviewed support is missing for VCP, cup-and-handle and low-float setups. Several popular "breakout" features point the wrong way. **Stocks with the largest one-day jump in the past month subsequently underperform** ([Bali, Cakici & Whitelaw via Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/hot-off-the-jfe-press-maxing-out-your-returns/)), so a stock that breaks out with one huge day is, by construction, in the losing group. Rising short sales are followed by negative returns ([Diether, Lee & Werner](https://users.nber.org/~confer/2005/mmf05/werner.pdf)). Machine learning does not rescue the approach either. The most credible model, a CNN that reads chart images, predicts 5- to 60-day returns, but its edge comes mostly from small caps and is measured before costs. Its value-weighted Sharpe ratio is about 0.6 ([Jiang, Kelly & Xiu via CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=34917)).

The track record of retail speculators is the base rate to beat. **Fewer than 1% of Taiwanese day traders are predictably profitable net of fees** ([Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finmar/v18y2014icp1-24.html)), and 97% of persistent Brazilian futures day traders lost money ([Chague, De-Losso & Giovannetti](https://repositorio.fgv.br/items/e87d04fc-4b4c-4a56-ab5a-1c84c1afde1b)).

### Twenty-two years of backtests leave one weak survivor

This project ran its own event study on 493 current S&P 500 stocks and 311 randomly drawn current small caps ($300M–$2B), using daily Yahoo bars from 2005 to October 2026. Entries were at the next open, costs were 10 bps round trip for large caps and 50 bps for small caps, and no share-price filter was applied ([engine.py](../research_code/breakout_backtests/engine.py)). Because both universes are today's survivors, **random entry alone beat SPY by +1.03% per 60 days in large caps** ([baseline.csv](../research_code/breakout_backtests/results/baseline.csv)). Beating SPY therefore proves nothing. The honest test is each signal's return minus the equal-weight return of the whole universe over the same window ("vs-EW"), with t-statistics computed on monthly averages so that overlapping holding windows do not inflate significance.

| Signal (large caps unless noted) | 60-day excess vs SPY | 60-day vs-EW, monthly (t) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| A: close above prior 52-week high | +0.71% (below the +1.03% random baseline) | −0.76% (−1.96) | No edge |
| B: A plus volume ≥2× the 50-day average | +1.38% | +0.14% (0.33) | Fails the conservative test |
| C: volatility contraction, then 20-day high on volume | +1.84% (2005–15), +0.08% (2016–26) | +0.19% (0.49) | Died after 2015 |
| D: gap up ≥4% on volume ≥3× average | +2.31% | +0.82% (1.91) | Weak survivor |
| Small caps, all four signals | Negative after 50 bps costs | None significant | Lose money |

Sources: [event_stats.csv](../research_code/breakout_backtests/results/event_stats.csv); [vsew_monthly.csv](../research_code/breakout_backtests/results/vsew_monthly.csv); [robustness splits in event_stats.csv](../research_code/breakout_backtests/results/event_stats.csv).

Signal D, the large-cap gap on heavy volume, survives, but with limits. Most of the move has already happened before a no-day-trade account can act. **D events gapped up a mean of +8.97% and added another +1.01% over SPY from open to close on day t, but only +0.09% the next day** ([D_dayt.csv](../research_code/breakout_backtests/results/D_dayt.csv)). Buying at the day-t open is not possible anyway, because the 3× volume condition is only known at the close. What a next-open entry captures is a slow drift of roughly +0.4% to +0.8% versus the equal-weight universe over 20–60 days. That drift is concentrated: **the top 10 of 454 tickers (CVNA, SMCI, TSLA, NVDA, COIN and others) supplied 65% of the summed excess**, and without them the t-statistic falls to 1.57 ([extras.txt](../research_code/breakout_backtests/results/extras.txt)). Several of those names joined the S&P 500 only because of the very runs being measured, so a universe built from historical index membership would likely show less. In 2016–2026 alone, the monthly vs-EW t-statistic is only 1.15. Computed directly from the event file, the median 60-day D trade actually **lagged the equal-weight universe by 0.32%**: the typical trade loses slightly, and the edge lives in a right tail of rare large winners ([events_large_D_h60.csv](../research_code/breakout_backtests/results/events_large_D_h60.csv)).

Trend-following portfolios fared no better. A Donchian 55/20 system on large caps looked excellent trade by trade: a profit factor of 1.65, an average win of +13.1% and an average loss of −6.4%. Its excess return versus SPY, however, was only +0.54% per trade, and +0.08% in 2016–2026 ([donchian_trade_stats.csv](../research_code/breakout_backtests/results/donchian_trade_stats.csv)). **A 20-slot portfolio compounded at 9.7% against SPY's 10.8%. A 5-slot version, about what $100 in $20 positions allows, made 8.4% with a −53.8% drawdown. In small caps it lost 2.6% a year with an −80.5% drawdown** ([donchian_portfolio.csv](../research_code/breakout_backtests/results/donchian_portfolio.csv)). With only five slots, luck in which signals happened to get funded moved annual returns (CAGR) across a 4.8–9.7% range. That spread is as large as any edge measured.

## News pays on day zero, before a next-morning order fills

The market prices news faster than a retail trader can react. SPY and E-mini futures prices move within about 5 milliseconds of macro surprises ([Chordia, Green & Kottimukkalur](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/353121)). The announcing firm's price jumps in **more than 90% of after-hours sessions that contain an earnings release** ([JFE "warp speed" study via Aarhus](https://law.au.dk/en/current/allnews/news/artikel/reagerer-med-lynets-hast-regnskabsmeddelelsers-afsmitning-paa-markedet)). FDA approval gains are centred on the event day, and after-close releases are realized at the next open ([Hamill et al. 2018](https://cora.ucc.ie/handle/10468/12099)). M&A targets gain about 35% (small caps) and 19% (large caps) on announcement ([Evidence Investor](https://www.evidenceinvestor.com/post/the-takeover-effect-what-it-is-and-how-to-benefit)), and about a quarter of the run-up comes before the announcement ([Radboud thesis](https://theses.ubn.ru.nl/handle/123456789/8393)). Post-earnings drift in large stocks has been **"non-existent since 2006"** ([Martineau, CFR 2022](https://cfr.ivo-welch.info/published/papers/martineau2021rest.pdf)). A 2025 paper that defends the drift still finds it insignificant once microcaps are excluded (t = 1.43) ([UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/is-post-earnings-announcement-drift-a-thing-again/)). The S&P 500 index-addition effect has shrunk from 7.6% in the 1990s to 0.8% ([Greenwood & Sammon, JF 2025](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w30748/w30748.pdf)). The practical consequence is that paying for speed buys nothing for this account. Free SEC EDGAR APIs (10 requests per second, no key) and end-of-day bars are enough ([SEC developer resources](https://www.sec.gov/developer)).

| Catalyst | What remains after a next-open entry | How to use it |
|---|---|---|
| M&A target, FDA decision, large-cap earnings beat | Almost nothing; priced on day 0 or overnight | Do not chase |
| Guidance issued with earnings | About 200 bps of drift until the next report ([Milian 2018](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/euract/v27y2018i1p105-128.html)) | Positive tag to log |
| Large move confirmed by same-direction estimate revisions | About 1–1.3% a month; moves *without* revisions tend to reverse ([Govindaraj et al.](https://alphaarchitect.com/2013/03/follow-the-trend-and-the-sell-side/)) | Positive tag to log |
| Opportunistic insider buying (Form 4) | 82 bps a month historically ([Cohen, Malloy & Pomorski](https://www.nber.org/papers/w16454)) | Candidate second signal |
| Analyst downgrade to sell | −9.1% over about 6 months ([Womack 1996](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v51y1996i1p137-67.html)) | Exit or avoid |
| "Strategic alternatives" announcement | +5.3% over 3 days, then −9.7% over 6 months ([RAS 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11142-025-09917-0)) | Hard exclusion |

The evidence on specific keywords is thinner than trading marketing implies. The durable finding concerns dictionaries, not magic phrases: **about three-quarters of words that general-purpose dictionaries tag as negative are not negative in finance** (tax, cost, liability, for example). Negativity measured with the finance-specific Loughran–McDonald list goes with lower returns on 10-K filing dates ([Loughran & McDonald, JF 2011](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=1331573)). Media pessimism pushes prices down temporarily, and then they revert ([Tetlock, JF 2007](https://www.columbia.edu/~pt2238/papers/Tetlock_Media_Sentiment_JF.pdf)). "Going concern" language has contested drift evidence ([Ogneva & Subramanyam, JAE 2007](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jaecon/v43y2007i2-3p439-452.html)). The research notes found no study that measures the return effect of the phrases traders most often scan for: "definitive agreement," "FDA approval," "raises guidance" or "offering." Large language models do predict next-day returns from headlines, but **the predictability is strongest in small caps and after bad news, and it declines as LLM adoption spreads**. One version reports cumulative returns falling from 350% at 10 bps costs to 50% at 25 bps ([Lopez-Lira & Tang, arXiv v6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.07619v6)). When the company is anonymized, the advantage shrinks out of sample, which implies that much of the in-sample edge came from look-ahead bias ([Glasserman & Lin](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17322)). A replication found that only a small part survives costs ([Vienna thesis](https://services.phaidra.univie.ac.at/api/object/o:2082913/download)). An LLM is therefore useful for tagging what kind of catalyst occurred, not as a source of alpha.

Attention is the main trap, and its damage plays out over exactly the days-to-weeks window this account holds. **The stocks Robinhood users bought most each day returned −3% abnormal over the next five days, −6% under extreme herding and −4.7% over 20 days** ([Barber, Huang, Odean & Schwarz, JF 2022](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/attention-induced-trading-and-returns-evidence-from-robinhood-use/)). The losses concentrated in stocks under $1B ([Evidence Investor](https://www.evidenceinvestor.com/post/why-most-robinhood-traders-earn-lousy-returns)). Google search spikes predict two weeks of higher prices and then a reversal ([Da, Engelberg & Gao](https://rady.ucsd.edu/faculty/directory/engelberg/pub/portfolios/GOOGLE.pdf)). WallStreetBets due-diligence posts lost their predictive power after GameStop ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/investing-expertise/wallstreetbets-contributors-and-users-skilled)). Investors who joined pump-and-dump schemes lost nearly 30% on average ([Leuz et al., NBER](https://nber.org/papers/w24083)). A news app built for Robinhood users that buys yesterday's most-mentioned gainer would reproduce the losing pattern that this research documents.

## IREN's +725% reflects ticker choice, not a repeatable rule

The post's own numbers make a weaker case than its headline. The post reports +725% for the strategy against +643% for buy-and-hold over 3.1 years. That works out to about 97.5% a year against 91%, or ending wealth of 8.25× against 7.43×, so the strategy finished only about 11% ahead. Nearly all of the strategy's return came from IREN's own rise. The buy-and-hold figures are plausible: over 25 September 2023 to 29 October 2026, this project measured IREN at **+616% with a −67.4% drawdown**, close to the post's +643% and −68.2%. The real question is what the same approach does on stocks that were not chosen after they soared. IREN's pivot from bitcoin mining to AI cloud hosting made it an extreme, news-driven winner over the window ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/how-can-irens-transition-ai-cloud-benefit-stock)).

To answer that question, the project applied one fixed daily rule to IREN and its peers. The rule bought at the next open after a close above the prior 50-day high and sold at the next open after a close below the 200-day EMA, with costs of 0.2% per side.

| **IREN** | **+142%** | **+616%** | −62.1% | −67.4% |

| APLD | −9% | +272% | −59.4% | −71.9% |

Source: [iren_peers.csv](../research_code/breakout_backtests/results/iren_peers.csv); rule in [iren_peers.py](../research_code/breakout_backtests/iren_peers.py). BITF returned no data.

**Across the 11 peers, the rule's median return was −19% against a median buy-and-hold of +159%. It lost money on 7 of 11 and beat buy-and-hold only on CORZ**, which has a short price history. IREN had the second-best buy-and-hold return in the group. Even on IREN, **one trade (+254.7%, June 2025 to March 2026) produced the entire gain**, while the other five returned between −20.1% and +2.1%. The drawdown reduction was real but inconsistent: shallower than buy-and-hold on 7 of 11 names. This test does not reproduce the marketed strategy, which used 30-minute bars. A 200-period EMA on 30-minute bars spans only about 15 trading days, so that version exits faster than the daily rule tested here, which could explain its smaller drawdown. Only the same peer-group test applied to the actual strategy would settle the question.

The statistics are also against the post. With 18 trades, one or two trades could account for the entire 82-point lead over buy-and-hold. Under the standard i.i.d. approximation, the standard error of an annualized Sharpe ratio over 3.1 years is about 0.7 ([SLCG practice note on Lo 2002](https://www.slcg.com/files/practice-notes/sharpe-ratio.pdf)), so the backtest cannot reliably tell a Sharpe of 0 from 1.5. "AI-built" implies that a tool searched many indicator, parameter and timeframe combinations. **Trying more than about 45 configurations on five years of data almost guarantees one with an in-sample Sharpe of 1 and an expected out-of-sample Sharpe of 0** ([Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253)), and the post does not disclose how many configurations were tried. Across 888 Quantopian algorithms, backtest Sharpe ratios explained less than 2.5% of live Sharpe ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/quantopians-academic-paper-about-in-vs-out-of-sample-performance-of-trading-alg/)). Bank strategies lost 73% of their median backtested Sharpe once live ([Suhonen, Lennkh & Perez via Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/?p=36892)). Two practical problems apply to this user specifically. Thirty-minute bars allow same-day exits, which this account forbids. And even the favorable −38.6% drawdown would take $100 to about $61. The SEC has settled "AI-washing" cases against advisers that overstated their AI capabilities ([Thomson Reuters Institute](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en/institute/articles/ai-washing-enforcement)). That is useful context for any post that sells an AI strategy on a single hand-picked ticker. **The claim shows, at most, that one rule found in-sample roughly matched a stock chosen because it had already soared**. It does not show consistent profitability.

## Robinhood's agentic account fences the money, not the decisions

Robinhood opened agentic trading in beta on **27 May 2026**, initially for stocks only ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/27/robinhood-now-lets-your-ai-agents-trade-stocks/)). The server at `agent.robinhood.com/mcp/trading` uses OAuth and can be added to Claude Desktop or Claude Code as a custom connector ([Vorp Labs](https://vorplabs.com/agent-tools/robinhood-cli)). Trades happen in a dedicated Agentic Account, and **the agent "only has access to the funds you deposit into that account"** ([Robinhood newsroom](https://robinhood.com/newsroom/robinhood-is-now-open-to-agents/)). Crypto was fully rolled out around 17 August 2026 ([Crypto Times](https://www.cryptotimes.io/2026/08/18/robinhood-rolls-out-agentic-accounts-for-crypto-trading/)), and built-in Robinhood Agents were announced on 29 September 2026 ([HOOD Summit](https://robinhood.com/newsroom/hood-summit-2026/)). Reviewers report no short selling, no leverage and no money movement ([Farao](https://getfarao.com/blog/robinhood-agentic-trading-review)). This is the only sanctioned route for an AI agent to trade equities at Robinhood. The unofficial alternatives log in with the user's full credentials, broke again when Robinhood changed its verification flow in July 2025 ([robin_stocks #1621](https://github.com/jmfernandes/robin_stocks/issues/1621)), and can trigger account locks ([go-trader #1818](https://github.com/richkuo/go-trader/issues/1818)). The user's choice of the official connector is sound.

What the connector does not do matters more. **For external MCP agents, per-trade approvals are off by default and "still rolling out"; they are on by default only for Robinhood's built-in agents** ([Robinhood support](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/trading-with-your-agent/)). The order tool, reportedly `place_equity_order`, places real orders with Agentic Account funds ([PolicyLayer](https://policylayer.com/tools/robinhood-agentic-trading.md)). The only general cap on trade size is the amount deposited ([Vorp Labs](https://vorplabs.com/agent-tools/robinhood-cli)). A connected agent can also **read all of your Robinhood accounts, including account numbers** ([Robinhood support](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/onboarding-an-external-agent/)), and that data then falls under the AI provider's terms. Robinhood "does not control, supervise, monitor, recommend, or audit these AI agents," and the user bears the losses ([Robinhood newsroom](https://robinhood.com/newsroom/robinhood-is-now-open-to-agents/)). Other brokers chose more conservative defaults: IBKR's connector only drafts orders ([Walnut](https://walnutinvest.com/resources/mcp-connectors-for-brokerages-compared)), and tastytrade requires a confirmation token before any order tool runs ([tastytrade docs](https://developer.tastytrade.com/docs/sdks-and-tools/mcp-server/)). The MCP specification says only that there "SHOULD always be a human in the loop," and tool annotations are hints to the client, not enforcement ([MCP spec](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2025-06-18/server/tools)). FINRA's 2026 oversight report singles out AI agents acting without human approval and exceeding their intended authority ([Debevoise](https://www.debevoise.com/insights/publications/2025/12/finras-2026-regulatory-oversight-report-continued)). Human approval therefore has to be designed in by the user. It is not a default.

Four unknowns matter specifically at $100. First, the `place_equity_order` input schema has not been published, and **third-party reports conflict on whether the MCP accepts fractional or dollar-amount orders**. One guide says "whole shares only" and that fractional positions break stop placement ([kevin1chun/robinhood-for-agents](https://github.com/kevin1chun/robinhood-for-agents); [Walnut help](https://walnutinvest.com/help/robinhood-agentic-account); [HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/how-to-build-an-autonomous-ai-trading-agent-on-robinhood)). If the answer is whole shares only, the assumption that any stock is buyable fails, because a $20 slot cannot buy a $200 share. Second, the supported time-in-force values and stop orders are also unknown. Third, eligibility is disputed: an X post says all US customers, while Finovate says Gold cardholders ([Robinhood on X](https://x.com/RobinhoodApp/status/2079195918595764300); [Finovate](https://finovate.com/robinhood-lets-agents-trade-stocks-and-make-payments/)). Fourth, the account type depends on the main account: Robinhood allows only one margin account per customer, so if the main account uses margin, the Agentic Account will be a cash account ([Walnut help](https://walnutinvest.com/help/robinhood-agentic-account)).

Account mechanics are straightforward. The pattern-day-trader rule ended on **4 June 2026** ([FINRA Notice 26-10](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/26-10)), and Robinhood implemented the change that day ([Robinhood support](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/day-trading/)). The user's no-day-trading rule is therefore self-imposed and stricter than the law. Leverage still requires $2,000 ([FINRA](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/intraday-margin-requirements)), so $100 trades unleveraged. A cash account cannot reuse unsettled sale proceeds, which settle the next trading day (T+1) ([Robinhood settlement](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/settlement-and-buying-power/)). Fractional orders must be at least $1 and are not executable in the after-hours session ([Robinhood Customer Agreement](https://cdn.robinhood.com/assets/robinhood/legal/Robinhood-Customer-Agreement.pdf)). Fixed costs are the silent killer: Robinhood Gold at $5 a month ([Robinhood](https://robinhood.com/us/en/support/articles/gold-overview/)) would consume 60% of the account each year. The wash-sale window spans 61 days and includes IRA purchases ([TaxAct citing IRS Pub. 550](https://www.taxact.com/support/602)). With 60-day holds and repeat signals on the same tickers, wash sales are likely and should be tracked. Personal use requires no registration. Sharing signals that auto-execute in other people's accounts is a different matter: an auto-trading arrangement got a newsletter publisher sanctioned as an unregistered adviser ([Weiss Research, Cahill memo](https://www.cahill.com/publications/firm-memoranda/0000001/_res/id=Attachments/index=0/CGR%20Firm%20Memo%20-%20In%20the%20Matter%20of%20Weiss%20Research%20Financial%20Newsletter%20Publisher%20Sanctioned%20as%20an%20Unregistered%20Investment%20Adviser.pdf)), and routing orders for transaction-based pay brought an unregistered-broker charge ([SEC v. Neovest](https://www.sec.gov/news/press-release/2021-113)).

## Build a signal journal with a human-signed order ticket first

The first build should be three small pieces around the existing connector, not a new connector. The first piece is a **nightly scanner** that runs after the close on free daily data. It flags signal D in liquid large caps, pulls the day's EDGAR filings (8-K items, S-3/424B offering documents, Form 4) and headlines, asks Claude to tag the catalyst type, and writes every event to a journal (SQLite or a spreadsheet), whether or not it is traded. The second is a **morning session in Claude with the Robinhood MCP**. It reads the journal, checks Agentic Account cash and positions through the read tools, drafts a plain-language order ticket for each eligible signal, and places only the orders a human approves, during regular hours. The third is a **weekly measurement report**. Before anything else, call MCP `tools/list` against the Robinhood server and read the order tool's schema: dollar amounts, limit and stop support, and time-in-force. That one check decides whether the five-slot fractional plan below is feasible.

| Setting | Version 1 rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Universe | Liquid large caps (S&P 500-type, at least $1M average daily dollar volume, fractional-eligible), with no share-price limit | Small-cap breakouts lost money after costs; liquid-stock effects persist |
| Entry signal | Day-t open at least 4% above the prior close and day-t volume at least 3× the 50-day average, scanned after the close | The only tested signal with a residual edge |
| Hard exclusions | "Strategic alternatives" language; an offering filing (S-3/424B) after the run-up | Documented reversals |
| Logged, not used | Guidance raise, confirming estimate revisions, insider buys, catalyst type | Hypotheses to evaluate later without adding trials now |
| Entry | Next regular session, within the first hour; never in extended hours | Fractional and extended-hours rules; day-t move already gone |
| Size | $20 dollar order per position, at most 5 positions, idle cash otherwise | Matches the tested 5-slot setup; keeps a $1+ order size |
| Exit | Sell proposal at 60 trading days; earlier only on a sell downgrade or an offering filing; no tight stop | The tested horizon; tight stops cut the right tail that carries the edge |
| Hard limits | At most 2 new orders a day, one per symbol, a collar on price deviation, a duplicate-order check, no same-session sells | Knight and Citi lessons; the no-day-trade rule |
| Kill switch | Equity at or below $80: halt entries and review; never top up mid-test | Drawdown limit at this scale |

The approval protocol needs three layers, because no single one is guaranteed. First, turn on Robinhood's in-app trade approvals for the MCP account as soon as they reach it. Second, never grant the client "always allow" on the order tool. Third, require the agent to present a ticket (symbol, dollars, order type, limit, signal date, catalyst tag, exit date) before calling the order tool. Hard limits must be blocks, not warnings. Knight Capital lost $460M after 97 error emails went unread ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/news/press-release/2013-222)), and Citi's trader overrode a pop-up on a $444bn basket ([MarketsMedia](https://www.marketsmedia.com/citi-fined-61-6m-for-fat-finger-trade/)). One discipline matters more than the others: **the human approves adherence to the rule, not the story**. Rejecting signals on gut feel adds an untested filter. Every rejection should therefore be logged so that approved and rejected signals can be compared later. FINRA's guidance for new algorithmic strategies is to pilot at limited size and scale only as results are confirmed ([FINRA Notice 15-09](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/15-09)). For $100, that means no added capital until the measurement plan below says yes.

Measurement is where the $100 account hits its real limit. In the event file, a 60-day D trade's excess return has a standard deviation of about 19.5%. Detecting a true 1% edge at t = 2 therefore needs roughly **1,500 trades**, and a 2% edge about 380 (computed from [events_large_D_h60.csv](../research_code/breakout_backtests/results/events_large_D_h60.csv)). Five slots held 60 days allow about 21 live trades a year, so the account's own profit and loss cannot distinguish skill from luck for decades. The universe produces about **160 D events a year**, so the journal's paper record of every signal is the real test. That record could confirm a 2% edge in about two and a half years, but would need nearly a decade for a 1% edge, and overlapping windows make even those estimates optimistic. Write the rules down before the first trade and freeze them for 12 months; any change counts as a new trial. Track four measures: (1) the mean return of all signals, taken and not, minus SPY and minus an equal-weight basket of the universe over identical windows; (2) slippage, as the actual fill against that day's open; (3) approval adherence, comparing approved and rejected signals; and (4) the live account against a $100 SPY shadow position. If after 12 months and at least 150 paper events the mean excess versus equal-weight is zero or negative, retire the signal. Even a positive result should not attract more money until about 400 events show t ≥ 2. The pre-committed fallback is a broad index fund, which beat every portfolio-level breakout implementation tested here on annual return.

## Conclusion

The $100 balance is not the binding constraint, and neither is the ban on day trading. Fractional shares remove the price barrier, and momentum is earned overnight anyway, so the no-day-trade rule forfeits only announcement-day jumps that a retail feed could not catch in time. The binding constraint is statistical power. A strategy whose typical trade slightly loses and whose edge sits in a few outsized winners cannot be judged from 20 trades a year, which is why the journal of every signal matters more than any order placed. The same logic dismantles the IREN post: a single hand-picked ticker is a sample of one, and the decisive question is always what the frozen rule did on the peers nobody would have advertised.

The broader implication concerns the connector itself. Robinhood's design solves the custody problem by walling off the money, but it leaves the decision problem to the user, with approvals off by default and full read access to every account. That reverses the risk a cautious builder should worry about. The danger is not losing more than $100. The danger is an agent that trades a plausible story quickly and often enough to make luck look like skill. A connector that makes each order slow, explicit, logged and measurable against a benchmark is the version that can actually show whether there is anything to scale.
